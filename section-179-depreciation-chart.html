Eligible vehicles by weight class

GVWR is the loaded weight rating on the door-jamb certification plate — not curb weight, and not what a spec site reports. It varies by trim, drivetrain and model year, so several vehicles below straddle the 6,000 lb line depending on configuration. Verify the plate before you buy. The 2026 numbers

§179 ceiling $2,560,000 Phases out dollar-for-dollar above $4,090,000 of total qualifying purchases; gone at $6,650,000. Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
Heavy SUV §179 cap $32,000 Applies to SUVs rated 6,001–14,000 lb GVWR. Prorated by business-use percentage.
Bonus depreciation 100% Made permanent by the OBBBA for property both acquired and placed in service after 19 Jan 2025. Not subject to the SUV cap.
§280F year-1 cap $20,300 The hard ceiling for any vehicle at or under 6,000 lb GVWR, bonus included. $12,300 without bonus. Rev. Proc. 2026-15.

What the chart doesn't show

  • Six bars, not five. Vehicles are five-year MACRS property, but the half-year convention splits the first year's depreciation, pushing the tail into a sixth tax year. "Five-year property" has never meant five tax years.
  • The bed-length rule beats the weight rule. A pickup over 6,000 lb GVWR escapes the $32,000 SUV cap only if its cargo bed is at least six feet of interior length. Most crew-cab short-bed configurations — the 5.5 ft F-150, the Gladiator, the R1T — are capped like an SUV despite being trucks.
  • §179 can't create a loss. The deduction is limited to aggregate business taxable income; the excess carries forward. Bonus depreciation has no such limit and can drive a loss, which is why it usually does the heavy lifting on an expensive vehicle.
  • Business use has to hold. Drop to 50% or below in any year of the recovery period and the accelerated deductions are recaptured as ordinary income. The chart assumes the stated percentage holds for all six years.
  • Placed in service, not purchased. The vehicle must be in service and available for business use by 31 December — a December order delivered in January is a next-year deduction.
  • State conformity varies. Several states decouple from bonus depreciation entirely and some cap §179 far below the federal ceiling, so the state result can differ sharply from the federal one shown here.
