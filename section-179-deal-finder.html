Buying the depreciation cliff

Section 179 doesn't care whether a vehicle is new or used — a two-year-old truck earns the identical 100% first-year write-off. So the play is to let the first owner absorb the cliff, then take the full deduction anyway. Ranked by dollars off original sticker, because that's the money — big percentages hide on cheap cars.

Read this before you chase the biggest number

  • A steep cliff is only useful if it flattens after you buy. The QX80 fell 49% because 2024 was the last year of the outgoing body — a redesign cliff, permanent and non-recovering. The Escalade barely moved because demand holds. You want the drop already taken, not still coming.
  • Percentage flatters cheap cars. The QX80's 49% is $38,796. The XM's 52% is $83,002. Same-ish percentage, twice the money, and one of them is a 644-hp V8 hybrid.
  • You still want resale. Writing basis to zero means the entire eventual sale price returns as §1245 recapture at ordinary rates — you keep roughly 63¢ of every resale dollar, not zero. Value retention after purchase still matters.
  • MSRPs here are base figures, excluding destination and options. Real stickers ran higher, so every discount shown is a floor. Pull the window sticker by VIN on finalists to see true as-built MSRP.
  • Plate-check anything marked verify. Those sit near the 6,000 lb GVWR line where trim and drivetrain decide whether you get the full write-off or the §280F cap — a swing of tens of thousands.
  • Two MSRPs came back unreliable and were excluded rather than guessed: 2023 Cadillac Escalade and BMW X7 base. Those models appear only where a trustworthy figure existed.
