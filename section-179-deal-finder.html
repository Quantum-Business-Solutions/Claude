Buying the depreciation cliff

Section 179 doesn't care whether a vehicle is new or used — a two-year-old truck earns the identical 100% first-year write-off. So the play is to let the first owner absorb the cliff, then take the full deduction anyway. Read this before you chase the biggest number

  • The XM's cliff is a warning as much as an opportunity. It fell 50% in three years because it was a first-model-year, low-volume plug-in V8 that the market never warmed to. Some of that decline continues after you own it. The Yukon Denali and Range Rover P400 held value precisely because demand is steady — you want the drop already taken, not still coming.
  • Percentage flatters cheap cars. Rank by dollars: a 26% drop on a $166K Range Rover Autobiography is $43,801, while 26% on a GLS 450 is $22,918. Same percentage, twice the money.
  • The Mercedes GLE sits on the line. Rated around 6,008 lb — roughly eight pounds of margin, and some configurations fall below 6,000. Get it wrong and a $52,695 write-off becomes a $20,300 §280F cap. Do not buy a GLE for this purpose without reading the door plate first.
  • You still want resale. Writing basis to zero means the entire eventual sale price returns as §1245 recapture at ordinary rates — you keep roughly 63¢ of every resale dollar, not zero. Value retention after purchase still matters.
  • MSRPs here are base figures, excluding destination and options. Real stickers ran higher, so every discount shown is a floor. Pull the window sticker by VIN on finalists to see true as-built MSRP.
  • Plate-check anything marked verify. Those sit near the 6,000 lb GVWR line where trim and drivetrain decide whether you get the full write-off or the §280F cap — a swing of tens of thousands.
  • You can settle GVWR from the VIN, free. NHTSA's vPIC API returns a GVWR class for any VIN with no key and no signup:
    vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/vehicles/DecodeVinValues/<VIN>?format=json
    The BMW XM came back Class 2F: 7,001–8,000 lb, which is how its verify flag was cleared — its §179 eligibility is now confirmed rather than assumed. VINs are published right on the cars.com search page, so this check is cheap on every candidate.
  • Rows are missing where MSRP couldn't be sourced, rather than guessed. The standard GMC Yukon Denali (non-Ultimate) has no published base figure I could verify, so six Denali listings sit outside this table — including a 2025 Yukon Denali at Camelback Ford, $75,590 with 11,663 miles, badged $11,000 below market. That's the largest below-market figure in the whole GMC set and worth a look on badge evidence alone.
  • Mercedes G-Class has no inventory here. Nothing within 250 miles under $140,000; the nearest is Las Vegas at 256 miles. A G-Wagon means shopping nationally with delivery.
