Contact Verification Process

How a purchased or inherited contact list becomes a list a rep can dial without apologising. Written from the full pass on list 5243: 662 contacts at intake, 491 read against dated LinkedIn history, 340 surviving. The other 171 left the list — on lead-status and persona writes this process itself made — before they were ever read.

input: one list ID source of truth: dated LinkedIn history output: verdict + channel companion: The Evidence Ladder
SCOPE

What this process is for, and what it refuses to do

A bought list decays from the day it is delivered. People change jobs, companies rebrand, vendors lag, and the CRM keeps serving the stale version with total confidence. This process reads every contact against dated employment history, records what it found and how it knows, and routes each person to a channel: dial them, re-target them at their new employer, or leave them alone.

It is deliberately narrow in one respect. It never upgrades a guess into a fact. Every write carries its evidence, every unverifiable record says so in plain language, and no field is filled just because a field was empty. A rep discovering the truth on a call is the expensive failure this exists to prevent; a blank field is the cheap one.

The one rule that generates all the others. Dated work_experience rows are evidence. A headline is marketing. A vendor record is a lead to a URL. A websites field is stale garbage. When two sources disagree, the dated row wins and the disagreement gets written down.

STAGE 1

Intake — read membership, not your notes

Pull the contact IDs from the list itself and re-read them after every write. A local queue file is a snapshot, and a snapshot goes stale the moment the first verdict lands.

This is not a stylistic preference. On list 5243 the queue file reported zero contacts remaining while thirty members had never been touched — they had no LinkedIn URL stored, so the queue-building step had silently skipped them. The list knew. The notes didn't.

GET /crm/v3/lists/{listId}/memberships?limit=250   → paginate on paging.next.after
POST /crm/v3/objects/contacts/batch/read           → 100 ids per call
Snapshot the intake membership before the first write, and keep it immutable. This process changes lead status and persona, and both are entry criteria — so its own writes eject records from the list it is working. On 5243 that removed 171 of 662 contacts before they were ever read, and only the intake snapshot makes them recoverable or even countable. Measure progress against live membership; measure coverage against the snapshot.
Completion is a property of the list, not of your loop. "Done" means every member carries a verdict when you read the flag straight off membership. Anything else is a guess about your own bookkeeping.
STAGE 2

Identify — three ways to find a human

Roughly one stored profile URL in seven is wrong or dead: a 422, a different person with a similar name, an abandoned duplicate profile with no dated history. Treat the stored URL as a hypothesis.

OrderMethodWhat it costsYield on 5243
1The URL already on the recordone profile read~85% usable
2ZoomInfo externalUrls — returns the profile URL directly, often severalone enrich callabout half the gaps
3LinkedIn people search on name + company keywordsone search + one readmost of the remainder
4Nothing worked → unreadable, naming every source tried—41 of 493, ~8%

When ZoomInfo hands back several LinkedIn URLs, try them in order — the first is frequently the dead one. Two contacts on 5243 were recovered exactly this way after their stored URL 422'd.

POST /api/v1/linkedin/search?account_id={acct}&limit=5
  {"api":"classic","category":"people","keywords":"Firstname Lastname Company"}
GET  /api/v1/users/{identifier}?account_id={acct}&linkedin_sections=experience_preview
Whose LinkedIn account reads the profile is not a detail. Both calls require account_id, and only the accounts belonging to your own team are authorised. A connected-accounts list will also contain client identities; reading prospect profiles through a client's LinkedIn account is not a recoverable mistake. Keep an explicit allowlist in the run config and fail closed on anything outside it.

Identifier hygiene, each learned by a 400 or a wrong answer: strip ?trk=… tracking parameters; URL-encode non-ASCII (ñ → %C3%B1); never send *experience as the section name. And read every property that might hold a profile URL, not just one — a portal typically carries several (hs_linkedin_url, a Hublead public identifier, a vendor's LinkedIn URL field). Reading one field is how 30 contacts got silently skipped on the first pass of this process.

STAGE 3–4

Read and judge

Signals, strongest first: a dated row whose end is null; the same company_id recurring across promotions; a start date consistent with the CRM. Then, far weaker: the headline. Then, not evidence at all: the websites field, which routinely still points at an employer three jobs back.

The three verdicts

VerdictMeansTest
yesStill at the company on filea dated row for that company with end: null
noGone — and usually somewhere identifiablethat company's row has an end date, or a different employer is current
unreadableCould not be established by any sourceno profile, or a real profile with no dated history
The truncation trap. experience_preview returns only the first handful of rows. If the CRM's company is absent and work_experience_total_count exceeds the rows you got, you have not seen the whole history — re-pull with linkedin_sections=experience before calling anyone a mover or a wrong human. Skipping this produces confident, wrong "no" verdicts.

Persona: the test is whether they can buy

Not being a marketer does not disqualify anyone. A CRO, an EVP of Partnerships, a VP of Communications, a COO promoted out of marketing — all buyers. What actually disqualifies: a gatekeeper (an executive assistant), an individual contributor with no budget, a one-person shop that cannot buy the offer, or an outside consultant.

Record that exclusion in the evidence field, never in lead status — a workflow will overwrite lead status, and should. See the failure modes below for the way this can go wrong.

STAGE 5

Movers — re-associate, don't just annotate

A contact confirmed to have moved is worth more than one confirmed to have stayed: they are a warm name at a new account. Finishing the job means the CRM shows where they actually work.

  1. Verify the domain, never guess it. Name searches fail or return garbage — a search for one small vendor returned an unrelated charity's domain. Instead, pass a candidate domain to a company lookup and accept only a full match on the right name.
  2. Decide whether there is a destination at all. Not every mover goes to a company you should create. Someone who went independent, took a fractional or advisory seat, is between roles, or retired has no employer to re-associate to — record the destination in the evidence field, leave the verdict no, and set the lead status to reflect it. Creating a company record for a one-person practice fills the CRM with junk and, worse, promotes a non-buyer onto the calling list.
  3. Disambiguate before you move anyone. A changed email domain looks like a job change and usually isn't — on one list, 37 of 49 domain mismatches were aliases or former names, not moves. And never choose between same-named company records: where several exist, suppress and queue rather than guess.
  4. Find or create the company record. Search by domain EQ first. Take the domain from the LinkedIn company page so the record is matchable later — never create a name-only stub.
  5. Delete the stale associations, then write the new one with both type IDs — 1 and 279. Write only one and associatedcompanyid stays empty, which looks like success and isn't.
  6. Reconcile the flag to yes. Once they are attached to the employer they actually work for, "still at company" is true. Leaving it no describes a record that no longer exists.
Creating a company record has a side effect. A record you create carries no tech-signal enrichment, so its occupants silently drop out of every ICP list keyed on those signals. Enrich on create, or accept that you are hiding the people you just finished verifying. This cost 27 verified contacts their place on the calling list.
STAGE 5b

Carry the phone, or the whole pass is undone

Re-associating a contact fixes the company, and a diligent pass then fixes the email. Both can be right while the record still connects a rep to the employer the person left — because business_phone was populated before anyone knew they had moved, and nothing in the re-association touches it.

This is the most damaging defect in the process, for one reason: the source list requires a known phone as an entry condition. So the stale number is not dormant data sitting in an unused field. It is the number a rep dials.

Measured on the 5243 movers: 69 of 70 carried a business number that was not their new employer's. 31 were proven to belong to a named former employer by matching the number against that company's own record — one CMO's business line was his previous employer's switchboard, and his filed previous email was on that same company's domain. Two independent fields agreeing on the wrong company.

You do not need to identify every number to know it is wrong

Proving ownership is satisfying and slow. The decisive argument is simpler: business_phone means the current employer's business line, and every value on a mover's record predates the discovery of the move. It therefore describes the previous job whether or not a company record exists to name it. Fix the field on that basis; use ownership lookups to build confidence, not as a precondition.

What to do with each field

FieldAction on a confirmed moverWhy
business_phoneOverwrite with the new employer's number; clear it if that company record has noneAn empty field costs a rep nothing. A wrong one costs a call and the credibility of the list.
phoneTouch only when proven to belong to a former employerOften a genuine direct dial that follows the person.
mobilephoneNever touchA mobile belongs to the person, not the employer.

Record the replaced number and whose it was in the evidence field. And check afterwards for contacts left with no dialable number at all — they will fall off a phone-gated list, which is correct, but somebody should know it happened.

Check the rest of the list too — and self-test the check before believing it

The same audit across all 312 contacts on the calling list found 276 matching their employer and 32 not. The first attempt to identify those 32 reported that none belonged to another company — and that conclusion was wrong. It used a digits-only token search that cannot match a number stored in parenthesised form, so it returned nothing for all 32 and the nothing was read as an answer. Re-run with a query that tries every stored format, and after a self-test against a number already known to be present, 9 of the 32 sit on a differently-named company's number.

Those 9 split two ways and neither the CRM nor a vendor can separate them automatically. Roughly half are the employer's own predecessor or an acquired subsidiary — a contact at Workiva holding the number registered to WebFilings, Workiva's original name. The rest look like a former employer's line on a contact who never moved, the same defect as the movers but pre-existing. The vendor alias test does not resolve this: it keeps predecessor names as separate company records with separate ids, so "different id" and "different company" are not the same statement once an acquisition is involved.

So non-mover phone conflicts get flagged for a human, never auto-corrected. Overwriting a contact's working office line with their employer's toll-free menu is a worse outcome than the ambiguity.

Self-test every query before you trust its silence. A digits-only token search against a parenthesised stored number returns nothing, for every input, forever. That silence was twice read as a finding in this pass — once as "no contact shares this number" and once as "no company owns this number" — and the second one reached a written conclusion before it was caught. Run the query against a case whose answer you already know. If it cannot find that, it has told you nothing about anything else.
STAGE 6

The email ladder

Vendors do not have the address of someone who just moved — that is precisely the population they lag on. Four rungs, in order, each cheaper than the one below it.

Rung 0 · Bounce history outranks the verifier

Before spending a credit, read what the CRM already knows about deliverability — the hard bounce reason, any sending tool's bounce flag, a stored verification result. A bounce is ground truth and no verifier overrides it: tested against three addresses that had genuinely hard-bounced, the verifier returned valid for two of them. Never re-check an address you already hold bounce data for, and never let a valid overturn a recorded bounce.

Rung 1 · Look in the record you already have

Sweep hs_additional_emails, email_2, email_other, work_email, linkedin__email for an address on the current employer's domain. Two of the four hardest cases on 5243 were solved here for one batch read — the correct address had been sitting in a secondary field while the primary held a dead one.

Two more fields worth reading at this rung, because they answer part of the question for free: a vendor's own stored verification status, and its “email matches company name” flag. And if the person's LinkedIn contact-info block carries a published address on the confirmed domain, prefer it over anything derived — it is first-party. On this pass a self-published gwen.lamar@ beat a vendor's gwendolyn.lamar@ when both verified.

Rung 2 · Learn the company's format from a colleague you already trust

Search your own portal for a known-good address on that exact domain, then score the samples against the format table below and keep only formats that explain a real sample. This solved 22 of 42 domains with no external calls at all.

POST /crm/v3/objects/contacts/search
  filter: email CONTAINS_TOKEN "*@thedomain.com"

Rung 3 · Construct across the universal format set, then make the server answer

There are only so many ways the world builds an address. Walk the set — learned formats first, then the rest — and let verification decide. Add common short forms of the first name once the full set fails on the given name.

Normalise the name before building a candidate. Decompose to ASCII, strip everything that is not a letter, lowercase. Without that step an apostrophe or an accent produces a malformed address — a derivation routine on an earlier run crashed on O'Dell, and in a bulk run it would have silently skipped every O'Brien, O'Neill and D'Angelo on the list. Skip contacts whose name does not yield two usable tokens rather than guessing.

Verification returns four answers, and they are not shades of one thing:

Rung 4 · Read the negatives

An invalid from a domain that answers is a finding, and it points two different ways:

  • The domain is wrong. Every format failed for one CMO at his company's current domain — the company had rebranded and kept its legacy mail domain, where first.last was valid all along.
  • The name is wrong. Every format failed for another contact, then one nickname variant succeeded: she goes by Kris, not Kristin. Five rejections and one acceptance from a discriminating server is a genuine answer.

The hard rule, and its two exceptions

An address is written only when its domain is the employer LinkedIn confirmed. A vendor-supplied address on the previous employer's domain is the exact failure this process exists to prevent. Two exceptions:

TierSituationBar to clear
1Same company, different domain — a rebrand keeping legacy mailthe vendor resolves both domains to the identical company id
2Parent group and division sharing one corporate mail domaindifferent ids, so weaker: requires valid plus a note recording the weaker tier

Where the old address goes

Never leave a former employer's address in the Email field, and never simply delete it.

previous__email
The prior address. Read it first — if it already holds a value, leave that value alone.
previous__company_domain_name
Its domain. URL-typed — a bare domain is rejected with INVALID_URL, so prefix https://.
ai__email_information
The narrative: what was replaced, what verification said, and how confident to be.
Clearing a primary address takes two writes, in order. Empty hs_additional_emails first, then empty email — otherwise the API refuses with "Remove all secondary emails first before deleting the primary email."
STAGE 7

Stamp, then let the automation do its job

Research that isn't written to a field does not exist. Every contact leaves with a verdict, the evidence behind it, the date, and how many sources agreed.

ai__li_still_at_company
yes · no · unreadable — the routing decision
ai__contact_evidence
What was read, where, and the caveat a rep needs before dialing
ai__contact_verified_date
When. A verdict without a date rots invisibly
ai__sources_confirming
1 for LinkedIn alone, 2 when a vendor independently agreed
Verify every write by reading it back. A batch update returns COMPLETE with zero errors and can still not apply. Diff the ids you requested against the ids returned, then re-read the records. Every self-check in this process exists because a write that reported success had not landed.
Calculated fields lag — do not mistake that for failure. associatedcompanyid can take 20 seconds or more to reflect a new association. Read it too early, see it empty, and you will "repair" a write that worked. Re-read before concluding anything failed. Related limit: a batch read will not accept propertiesWithHistory at 100 records — drop to 25 per call or the whole response comes back null. Property history is the only way to catch two systems fighting over one field, so this matters more than it looks.
The lead-status workflow is not fighting you. Writing an email triggers it, and it asynchronously returns the contact to prospect status at their new company — which is the entire point: information was found, so they are workable again. Do not suppress it, do not re-assert a stale status inside the same write, and do not trust a status you read before the write. Re-read it afterward if you need to know.

The portal enum offers two dozen values; only the four above are outputs of this process.

The evidence field has one writer's worth of room and several writers

Roughly 1,000 characters, hard truncated. On this pass the verdict writer overwrote the field while every later phase appended to it — which is why 22 of 70 movers lost the marker two production lists filter on, and why a repair script had to exist. Make every write append, give each phase a size budget, put markers in dedicated typed properties rather than prose, and run any marker re-stamp after the last verdict write.

Properties this process must not write

  • jobtitle — three systems already fight over it at roughly 38% oscillation; a write was observed reverting within seconds. The real title belongs in the evidence field.
  • hs_persona — see the next section. Proposals only.
  • mobilephone — a mobile follows the person, not the employer.
  • Anything on a contact outside the intake snapshot.
OUTPUT

The calling list, defined

The process produces a second, derived dynamic list — the one reps actually work. It is worth stating its clauses exactly, because three of the four are properties this process writes, which means the process can remove people from its own output.

ClauseFilterNote
1ai__li_still_at_company IS_ANY_OF [yes]confirmed current
2hs_lead_status IS_ANY_OF [ConnectandSell Prospect]this is why clause 2 of the vocabulary table matters
3ai__contact_evidence DOES_NOT_CONTAIN <exclusion token>must set includeObjectsWithNoValueSet: true, or every contact with an empty evidence field drops out
4IN_LIST <source list id>inherits the whole source ICP — the coupling that hides verified people

Clause 4 is the one to think hardest about. It keeps the calling list honest to the campaign's ICP, and it is also why re-associating a mover can make them vanish: they leave the source list, so they leave this one. Decide deliberately whether your output should inherit the source ICP or stand on the verification evidence alone.

PERSONA

Persona: the largest destructive write, and the one to default off

On this pass, correcting hs_persona removed 105 of the 324 contacts that left the source list — more than any cause except the verdicts themselves. Those removals were right: the records were analysts, sales directors, a COO, a marketing specialist, people who were never marketing buyers and had been mis-personad into the campaign. But it is a write to a targeting property, it is the second-largest thing this process does, and it deserves to be named as a stage rather than appear as a number in a table.

In an unattended run this defaults to off, and produces proposals. A persona re-map changes who is in the campaign. Emit the proposed change with the title that justifies it and let a human approve the set.

What makes it treacherous

  • Multiple persona workflows race each other. Expect a value you write to be re-decided. Verify by reading back.
  • The workflow that assigns "does not match persona" only fires on a BLANK persona. A wrong persona is therefore stable — nothing will correct it for you, and nothing will correct yours either.
  • Persona is title-driven, so it survives a job change at the same title. If movers are dropping out of a persona-gated list, persona is probably not the reason — check which criterion actually failed before concluding anything.
FAILURE MODES

Two ways a perfect record still fails to reach a rep

Both of these were found after the list was reported complete, which is the argument for verifying against the list rather than against your own bookkeeping.

A filter token is a control character, not prose

The calling list excludes anyone whose evidence field contains a persona-exclusion token. When seven people were correctly reinstated as decision makers, the note explaining the reinstatement quoted that token verbatim — so the filter kept matching and seven qualified prospects stayed invisible for hours. Write the token only when you mean it, build it at runtime so a script file cannot seed it either, and phrase every removal note without it.

Re-association can walk a contact off the source list

The calling list gates on membership in the source list, which requires a known phone, a qualifying persona, and membership in two tech-signal lists. Move someone to a new employer and they can leave the ICP — which is often correct. The distinction that matters is between disqualified and unexamined:

  • Disqualified. An enriched company genuinely lacking the signal. An accurate record in the wrong campaign — leave it.
  • Unexamined. A company record created during the pass, never enriched, whose signals are unknown rather than absent. These are verified prospects being silently dropped, and they are recoverable by enriching the company.

Persona is title-driven, so it survives a job change at the same title — persona was not what removed the movers. Check which criterion actually failed before concluding anything.

LISTS

Turning the output into lists people can use

The obvious filter is the wrong one. "Primary associated company has been updated in the last 30 days" looks like it answers "who changed jobs" and does not — it reports CRM activity, not employment change. It fires on a dedupe, a merge, an integration sync, someone correcting a bad link, and on this process's own re-association writes. Nothing in it requires that a human being actually moved, or that anyone checked.

On a portal with 138,000 contacts carrying a company association, that filter returned 615 people for a 30-day window. The evidence-based question — verified to have changed employer in the same window — returned 192. The gap is plumbing noise, and a rep working it burns calls discovering that nothing changed.

Ask the evidence, not the audit trail

Two branches, joined with OR, each requiring that something was actually established:

BranchFilterMeans
Aai__contact_evidence CONTAINS "RE-ASSOCIATED"we moved their primary company after confirming the move
Bai__li_still_at_company = noconfirmed gone; destination not set yet
+ai__contact_verified_date within the windowscopes either branch to a period
A marker only works if every record carries it. 22 of 70 movers were missing the RE-ASSOCIATED string because later evidence writes had overwritten the phrasing. A list keyed on a marker is only as complete as the marker — audit coverage before trusting the count, and re-stamp the gaps.

The two lists worth standing up

ListDefinitionWhat it is for
Moved companiesevidence CONTAINS "RE-ASSOCIATED"Warm names at new accounts. The single highest-value output of a verification pass.
No primary associated companynumber_of_associated_companies = 0, including no-valueOrphans. Nothing about them can be scoped by company, so they fall out of every ICP list silently.

List-filter shapes the API will not guess for you

The list endpoint rejects malformed filters with the valid enum in the error message, so probing beats reading. Four findings that each cost a round trip:

  • associatedcompanyid is hidden and cannot be used in a list filter at all — "Property filters cannot be used against hidden properties." Use number_of_associated_companies, a visible rollup, and set includeObjectsWithNoValueSet: true so records that never had one are caught alongside those at zero.
  • A date property accepts only COMPARATIVE_PROPERTY_UPDATED, TIME_POINT, TIME_RANGED, ALL_PROPERTY. Not DATE, and not ROLLING_DATE_RANGE — that one is for datetime properties.
  • A rolling window is a TIME_POINT with timeType: "INDEXED", an indexReference of referenceType: "TODAY", and a positive offset — the API rejects negative offsets even though the window looks backwards.
  • The timezone is not yours to choose. zoneId must be the portal's own — "Invalid time zone UTC provided, valid time zones are America/Chicago."

An absolute cutoff is the pragmatic fallback: build the list with a fixed date and switch it to a rolling window in the UI, which is one click and immune to all of the above.

NEXT LIST

Running this again

The process takes a list ID and nothing else, but three things are worth settling before you start.

  • Write after every batch, never at the end. Reps dial while the pass runs. A verdict held in memory protects nobody.
  • Run verification concurrently and in the background. Six workers, and expect a serial loop over forty contacts to blow a two-minute shell timeout.
  • Decide the reachability bar up front. A cold prospecting list and a deal-follow-up list fail differently; the bar is a parameter, not a constant.
  • Phones are still unverified. Every contact on the calling list has a number and nobody has proven it dials. For a list built for dialing, that is the largest remaining gap in this process.
