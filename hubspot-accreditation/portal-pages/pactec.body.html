The situation

PacTec runs a three-system revenue stack: Salesforce as the CRM of record, ZoomInfo for prospect data and buyer intent, and HubSpot for marketing and demand generation. The three were not exchanging usable data.

Use case 1 — Salesforce integration

The problem

  • Salesforce→HubSpot activity sync had stopped on 5 February 2026 and the cause had never been established
  • Deal sync had never been configured at all, so marketing had no visibility of revenue outcomes and no basis for attribution
  • Fourteen standard Salesforce integration properties were missing from HubSpot, silently breaking field mapping and reporting

The solution, and what we chose not to do

The integration was designed under a strict constraint: Salesforce remains the system of record. The governing question was therefore not how much data could be synchronised, but what deliberately must not be — protecting Salesforce from HubSpot-side noise while giving marketing the revenue visibility it lacked.

Alternatives considered. A middleware or iPaaS layer was assessed and rejected for this use case: the native integration met the object and directional requirements, and inserting middleware between two systems that already had a reliability problem would have added a failure point rather than removed one. Separately, an upgrade to Salesforce integration version 2 was researched and its duplicate-record exposure assessed before any decision — not upgraded blind.

The design decision that defines this build

Rather than replicating 423 intent properties into Salesforce, we built a single condensed custom field — HubSpot Summary — mapped bidirectionally, carrying the intent narrative into the Salesforce record where the seller actually works. Field sprawl traded for usable context, then validated end to end with live test contacts before release.

How it was implemented

  • Connector installed; object alignment defined across contacts, leads, deals, tasks and activities
  • Field mapping, sync rules and inclusion criteria configured; selective sync with explicit decisions on what must not synchronise
  • Pipeline stages aligned between the two systems
  • Salesforce-sourced HubSpot properties created; fourteen missing standard integration properties gap-filled
  • The 5 February 2026 failure root-caused and the activity sync restored, with historical activity data imported to backfill the gap
  • Salesforce→HubSpot deal sync wired for the first time
  • HubSpot→Salesforce task creation investigated, remediated, then extended to dual routing for intent triggers
  • Custom HubSpot Summary field mapped and validated end to end with live test contacts

Use case 2 — ZoomInfo marketplace integration

The problem

PacTec was paying for ZoomInfo buyer intent that could not reach a seller. No property model to receive the signals, no segmentation to act on them, no alerting, and no path from an intent spike to a Salesforce opportunity. The spend could not be justified because no line connected a signal to revenue.

What was built

ComponentDelivered
Intent property model47 intent topics deployed as 423 HubSpot properties — five properties for each of 48 contact-level signals, plus four company-level properties
Data exportAutomated ZoomInfo→HubSpot export; the same property model then replicated into Salesforce alongside the client's administrator
TargetingZoomInfo saved searches aligned to the defined ideal customer profile
AlertingIntent signal → HubSpot workflow → real-time sales alert
Cross-system pathZoomInfo prospect → HubSpot lead → Salesforce opportunity, tested end to end with dummy contacts before go-live
PersonasFive personas with properties, segments and workflows: Head of Organization, Head of Operations, Head of Procurement, Industrial Waste Management, Nuclear Waste Management
Clustering30 intent cluster lists — 15 marketing email and 15 master; Facilities Management activated as pilot
Commercial governanceZoomInfo subscription audited for seats against bulk credits; account-level intent delivery escalated to ZoomInfo support
ReportingIntent dashboard — volume, clusters, customer vs non-customer — plus an intent-to-revenue attribution process

The clustering model matters: sellers receive coherent themes rather than forty-seven separate signal types. That is the difference between intent data being available and intent data being used.
