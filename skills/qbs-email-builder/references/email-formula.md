# Email Formula

Every email has 6 elements. Get each right and the email converts. Skip any one of them and performance drops.

## The 6 elements

1. **Subject line** — the open trigger
2. **Preview text** — the inbox-companion to the subject (extends curiosity, doesn't restate)
3. **Eyebrow label** — small caps text above the headline; signals what this is
4. **Headline** — the in-email H1; restates the subject in a slightly more complete form
5. **Body** — 140–180 words, one framework, opinionated voice
6. **CTA** — soft question in body, button below

## Element 1: Subject line

Patterns that work for B2B (validated against real performance data):

- **Statement of contrarian opinion**: "Your CRM is lying to you about pipeline"
- **Specific pain + specific number**: "5 HubSpot features 90% of customers never turn on"
- **Honest take framing**: "Honest take: AI SDRs — what's real, what's hype"
- **Lowercase, conversational**: "quick one — sequences vs workflows"
- **Question + implication**: "Why most B2B emails go ignored (and what works)"

Patterns to avoid:

- ALL CAPS or starting with a capital letter for emphasis
- Multiple exclamation marks
- Words flagged by spam filters: FREE, ACT NOW, GUARANTEED, LIMITED TIME, RISK-FREE, $$$
- Generic intro phrases: "Welcome to...", "Introducing...", "Are you ready for..."

Subject length sweet spot: **6–10 words**. Long enough to be specific, short enough to render fully on mobile.

## Element 2: Preview text

The preview text appears next to the subject in most inboxes. Most companies waste it by leaving it blank or letting it auto-fill from the email's first line.

**Use it strategically:**

- **Extend** the subject's curiosity gap, don't restate it
- 50–90 characters fits in most inboxes without truncation
- Match tone to subject (don't be formal in the preview if the subject is conversational)

Examples:

| Subject | Good preview | Bad preview |
|---|---|---|
| Your CRM is lying to you about pipeline | If your forecast keeps missing, the problem isn't your reps. | This email is about CRM data quality. |
| 5 HubSpot features 90% never turn on | You're paying for them. Here's how to get ROI this week. | Learn about HubSpot features in this email. |

## Element 3: Eyebrow label

The eyebrow is small uppercase text above the headline. It tells the reader what kind of email this is and creates a recognizable header pattern across the program.

Patterns:
- **"A POV from [Name]"** — for opinionated takes from a specific person
- **"From the [Company] test lab"** — for data-backed insights
- **"Behind the curtain"** — for behind-the-scenes content
- **"[Topic] in practice"** — for tactical how-to emails
- **"An urgent POV"** — for risk/timely content

Keep eyebrows under 6 words. Consistent typography across the program (small caps, letter-spaced, gold or muted color).

## Element 4: Headline

The in-email headline is your second chance to convince the reader to keep reading. It should:

- Be a more complete version of the subject (if subject is "Your CRM is lying," headline can be "Your CRM is lying to you about pipeline — here's the fix")
- Use serif font (Instrument Serif default for QBS)
- Be 38px-ish for desktop readability
- Single sentence, no period if it's punchy enough to stand alone

Avoid headlines that start with:
- "How to..."
- "The ultimate guide to..."
- "5 ways to..."
- Any number-listicle pattern (the subject already used this)

## Element 5: Body

Length: **140–180 words**. This range matched the highest-converting emails in QBS history. Going longer almost never improves performance — it just gives more chances to lose the reader.

Structure inside the body:

1. **First-name greeting** ("[firstname],")
2. **Opening hook** — 1-2 sentences that name the problem or stake a claim
3. **One framework** — numbered list, contrast pair (what works vs. what doesn't), or before/after
4. **Tightening line** — one sentence that drives the insight home
5. **Soft CTA question** — "Want me to walk through your portal?" or "Curious how this looks for your team?"

What to avoid in body:

- Multiple frameworks (pick one — emails with 2+ frameworks underperform)
- Long paragraphs (4+ lines) — break into shorter paragraphs
- Hedging language ("might," "could," "perhaps") — opinionated voice converts better
- More than 1-2 emoji per email
- Heavy formatting (bold/italics) on every other word

## Element 6: CTA

For the QBS approach, default to a **meeting CTA** — gold button below the body that goes to a meeting link.

Button text patterns:
- "Book a [topic] consultation →"
- "See how we [verb] →"
- "Get a [thing] →"

Always include the arrow `→` — it lifts CTR consistently.

For tactical emails where a soft reply works better than a meeting:
- Body ends: "Reply with [KEYWORD] and I'll send the [thing]"
- Button is secondary: "Or book a call →"

The keyword pattern lets the user filter their inbox by the keyword and route or auto-respond.

## A complete example

```
EYEBROW: A POV from Shawn Peterson
SUBJECT: Your CRM is lying to you about pipeline
PREVIEW: If your forecast keeps missing, the problem isn't your reps. It's your CRM logic.
HEADLINE: Your CRM is lying to you about pipeline — here's the fix.

BODY (143 words):
[firstname],

Every CRO I've ever worked with has the same quarterly ritual:

Pull the pipeline report. Stare at it. Cut it in half. That's the "real" number.

Why do we do this? Because we don't trust the CRM.

The fix isn't a new tool. It's fixing the 3 things your HubSpot is getting wrong:

🎯 Deal stages tied to buyer actions, not seller hope
🎯 Exit criteria for every stage (what MUST be true to advance)
🎯 Weighted probability that actually reflects reality

When we implement this for clients, forecast accuracy jumps 20-40 points inside one quarter. Not because the pipeline changed — because it finally tells the truth.

Want me to walk through your pipeline with you?

CTA: Get a Free Pipeline Diagnosis →
```

That's the complete formula. Apply it to every email in the program.

## Voice matching for client work

When writing for a client (not QBS), the formula stays the same but the **voice tokens** change:

- Match the sentence rhythm of their best-performing email
- Match their use of "we" vs. "I"
- Match their level of formality (some clients are casual, others are buttoned-up)
- Match their vocabulary (industry terms they use, terms they avoid)
- Match the signature style (some clients sign as the company; some sign as a person)

If their voice is genuinely different from QBS's, you may need to adjust framework choices too — a buttoned-up enterprise client may hate the contrarian framing that works for QBS. Always match the company you're writing for.
