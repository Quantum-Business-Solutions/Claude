Big idea, positioning, and campaign name

The big idea

Every sales team has a guy named Bob. Nobody hired him. He just shows up: in the voicemail queue, in the spreadsheet that's locked for editing, in the follow-up you forgot to send, in the file called Final_Final_v3. He isn't lazy. He's busy. He keeps you busy too.

The campaign gives busywork a face, so owners and managers can finally talk about it. Once busywork is a person, you can do something about him: spot him, count him, and show him the door.

QBS is the team that helps you leave Bob behind, one rung at a time:

Rung What it does How Bob loses
1. Assistant You ask, it answers Bob can't bury you in 3 hours of account research
2. Automation A trigger runs it Bob can't make you type the same follow-up nine times
3. Agent It decides, you approve Bob can't keep you up at night with 200 unread emails

Positioning line

QBS helps sales and service teams get their selling time back, by moving the busywork to an assistant, an automation, or an agent, one rung at a time.

Short version for bios and ads: "Same job. Same hours. Different system."

Why it works for this audience

  • Office-technology dealers are practical, a bit skeptical of AI hype, and have seen a lot of vendors come through. Humor gets past the eye-roll. A villain they recognize gets remembered.
  • Bob is funny, not mean. He's the old way, not the old guy. The story never makes fun of veteran reps; Rick is the hero who gets out.
  • The film sells the outcome (time back, the kid's game) rather than the software.

Campaign name options

# Name Pros Cons
1 Leave Bob Behind Matches the end card; it's a verb, so it works as a CTA; easy hashtag (#LeaveBobBehind) "Bob" needs context the first time you see it
2 Bog Down Bob Character-first, memorable, great for the episode series Names the problem, not the fix
3 Same Job, Different System Clear and business-like; good for execs Less fun; sounds like a white paper
4 The Busywork Breakup Pulls from the best scene; strong emotional hook Works best on social, weaker on a sales page
5 Is Bob in Your Org? A question, so it pulls people into the quiz Better as the lead magnet name than the umbrella

Recommendation: Use Leave Bob Behind as the campaign name and hashtag. Use Bog Down Bob as the name of the recurring character series ("Bob Sightings"). Use "Where's Bob in your org?" as the quiz name. Keep "Same job. Same hours. Different system." as the positioning line on every end card.

Name check: none of these use a third-party trademark. We do not call Bob a "Mayhem" character anywhere public. That's an internal creative reference only.

Interactive and recurring ideas

6.1 "Bob Sightings" series

Short episodes (:15 to :45, vertical first) where Bob shows up in a new, very specific busywork moment. Same formula every time: Bob's line, the pain, a 2-second flash of the "Quantum way," the "Done." button.

Sighting Bob's line The Quantum way flash
Expense reports "Retype it. The photo might be lying." Receipt photo, auto-filled, submitted
CRM data entry "Type it in. Then type it in again over here." Voice note becomes a logged activity
The meeting about the meeting "Let's set up a quick sync to plan the kickoff." Agenda and notes generated; meeting cancelled
Reply-all storm "Please remove me from this thread. Reply all." Summary bot posts one line
Service ticket handoff "Did anyone tell sales the lease is ending?" Automation alerts the rep 120 days out
Onboarding a new rep "Here's a binder. And my cell." AI assistant trained on the playbook

Invite the audience in: "Where did you see Bob this week?" Best replies become the next episode (with credit, if they agree).

6.2 Lead magnet: "Where's Bob in your org?" quiz

Format: 12 questions, 2 minutes, built as a HubSpot form or a simple quiz page that writes to HubSpot. Results page shows score instantly; email gate for the full PDF report (name, email, company, role, team size).

Structure: 6 Bob Zones, 2 questions each. Each zone maps to a cluster in QBS's existing 137-task matrix of sales and marketing tasks, so every answer translates to named tasks and a rung.

Zone Maps to matrix category Sample questions
1. Prospecting Prospecting and outreach tasks "How do reps find who to call today?" / "What share of dials reach a live person?"
2. Research Account and contact research "How long does it take a rep to prep for a first meeting?" / "Where does account intel live?"
3. Follow-up Sequences, tasks, reminders "How are follow-ups triggered?" (memory / sticky notes / calendar / automated sequence) / "How many follow-ups were missed last month?"
4. CRM and pipeline Data entry, pipeline hygiene, reporting "When are meetings logged?" (never / end of week / same day / automatically) / "Could your manager forecast from the CRM alone?"
5. Meetings Notes, recaps, internal meetings "Who takes notes in customer meetings?" / "How many internal status meetings a week?"
6. Contracts and renewals Lease/contract tracking, competitive displacement "How do you know when a competitor's contract is ending?" / "Where are your contract files?"

Scoring: Each answer scores 0 (the Quantum way) to 3 (full Bob). Maximum 36 raw, converted to a Bob Score out of 100 (raw / 36 x 100). Higher is more Bob.

Bob Score Label What the results page says
0 to 24 Bob Who? "You've mostly left Bob behind. Your next step is Rung 3: agents that decide, with you approving."

9.2 AI-generated video disclosure

  • If the film or any cutdown uses AI-generated or AI-altered people, voices, or scenes that look realistic, turn on the platform's disclosure:
  • YouTube: check "Altered or synthetic content" in YouTube Studio on upload. That's required for realistic synthetic content.
  • LinkedIn: LinkedIn shows content credentials (C2PA) when files carry them; don't strip that metadata. Add a plain line in the post or first comment: "Made with the help of AI video tools."
  • Instagram / TikTok: use the "AI info" / "AI-generated" label toggles.
  • Add a line to the YouTube description and the landing page: "This film was made with AI-assisted production. Characters are fictional. The scoreboard is a dramatization."
  • Own it. For a company that sells AI, "we made this with AI" is a feature. Consider a short behind-the-scenes post: "How we made Bob."
  • Do not use AI to generate likenesses of real, identifiable people (other than cast members who've signed releases).
