UBEO Buried Intelligence

UBEO's CRM already knew when thousands of prospects' copier leases end and who signs for them. None of it was in a field anyone could filter. It was in call notes, in task headers, and — for about sixteen hundred contacts — typed into the contact's name. This is the record of what was found, what was built, and what was tested and rejected.

Findings

Each of these came out of reading real records. The quoted material is exactly as stored.

Finding 01 · contact name fields

The team has been recording decision makers inside contacts' names

With no field to hold it, reps typed the answer into the only editable box in front of them. Roughly 1,600 contacts carry a hand-written marker in firstname or lastname.

firstname: "Tomiko (DM)" lastname: "Hankerson"

firstname: "Chris" lastname: "Leitner (DM)"

firstname: "(Retired) Nancy" lastname: "McCorkle"

firstname: "DO NOT EMAIL Katherine" lastname: "Scardino"

lastname: "Mansu (DM) (Replaced Tami F)"

Live contact records, UBEO portal 516382

That last one records a succession — Leeah Mansu replaced Tami Forester — in a surname. Three consequences: none of it can be filtered or reported on; markers in firstname render inside email greetings; and any enrichment tool that writes the name field destroys it silently.

Recovered mechanically in two minutes: 2,088 contacts. Names were not modified — only the new AI fields were written, so the migration is safe to re-run and nothing downstream breaks.

Every logged attempt at that account went to Linda, who has no authority — which is why it shows years of polite declines. Trish decides, and appears in no call log.

Finding 03 · rep shorthand

Reps write the answer down; it converts better than the AI notes

29% of determinations came from the rep's own handwritten note rather than the AI-generated summary — and those convert at 73% versus 62% overall. Portal-wide, 9,299 distinct engagement records contain explicit decision-maker language.

The last line is why this cannot be automated with pattern matching: it is an explicit negative on a named person, and a regex reads it exactly backwards.

Finding 04 · everything read

Byproducts nobody asked for

  • 1,610 contacts marked as no longer at the company, plus 281 retired — still on call lists, most with live email addresses.
  • 16 opt-out and do-not-call requests recorded in free-text notes, where no suppression process can see them.
  • Buying structure — accounts that cannot decide locally at all: state procurement, district contracts, parent companies, purchasing committees, fully outsourced IT. Working these as normal prospects is wasted effort.
  • Incumbent providers named in passing — Ricoh, Xerox, Toshiba, Konica Minolta, Pacific Office Automation, OfficeTech, Office Value, Conway.
  • Live meetings and callbacks sitting unactioned in old notes, including one booked onsite meeting whose date is recorded two different ways on the same record.

The recommendation

Stop expanding either list. Point the reading at the 702 accounts that already have a clock running — verify the named contact at each, and send the 228 with nobody to call to enrichment with a deadline attached. That is a bounded job measured in days, and every determination lands on an account that is actually in play.

Tested and rejected

Recorded so nobody rebuilds them. Each was measured against records where the answer was already known.

Inferring authority from how the contact speaks

A buyer gives themselves away — "we went with Ricoh", "I handle the copiers", "send me a proposal". Built as a scorer and validated against 220 hand-read calls: it found 0 of 16.

The cause is structural. UBEO's call notes are AI-generated summaries, not transcripts, so first-person speech is paraphrased into the third person before it is ever stored — 0 of 220 calls contain first-person authority language, against 91 of 220 with third-person. Rebuilt as coreference it reached 26%, failing by extracting who, person who, she and staff members as names. Verbatim tasks were tested too: one relevant hit in 96 records. Useful for ranking a reading queue; never for filling a field.

Classifying decision makers by job title

856,722 contacts carry a job title and roughly half hold one that implies buying authority. It would produce about 418,000 labels — and it is a job-title filter, not intelligence. It cannot distinguish a VP at a 50,000-person firm from an office manager who actually signs, it inherits stale titles, and it says nothing about timing. Available if wanted as a clearly separate confidence tier; not run.

Mining the legacy SalesStrategy field

Present on 178,240 migrated tasks. A 60-record sample contained zero decision-maker statements — it holds revenue boilerplate, equipment models and meeting times. The single useful value found was already caught by the ordinary language sweep.

Open questions for UBEO

What are scrole1 through scrole6?

Six Yes/No fields on 30,987 contacts, carrying roughly 5,200 Yes values (scrole1: 1,878 · scrole4: 1,677 · scrole6: 698 · scrole2: 342 · scrole3: 312 · scrole5: 297). The labels say nothing — "SCRole3". They appear to have come across in the SalesChain migration. If any one of them means "decision maker", that is several thousand more at no cost. Somebody who worked in the legacy system will know in seconds.

Should the name fields be cleaned?

The markers have been copied into proper fields, so the intelligence is safe either way. Cleaning the names themselves would fix the email-rendering problem — a contact stored as DO NOT EMAIL Katherine is currently marketable with a live address — but it edits data the team put there deliberately. That is UBEO's call, not ours.

Buying roles on contact records

hs_buying_role is set on 2 contacts out of 1,089,827 and has been left untouched throughout, by instruction. There are now 284 identified decision makers whose records could populate it. Writing it changes data live workflows may depend on, so it needs an explicit decision.

Method, in brief

Six stages, with quality gates between them. Signals are extracted from unstructured engagement text, tiered by how firmly the evidence supports them, written to the engagement record with the quote and source id attached, then rolled up to the company and contact. Nothing overwrites better data, and current customers are excluded at both the engagement and rollup level.

Things that cost real accuracy

  • The search cap. HubSpot silently truncates at 10,000 results. Without recursive date-windowing the first pass was 36× too small and gave no warning.
  • Direction inversion. "a year and a half left" and "a year and half into their contract" differ by one word and three years. Elapsed-term language was invisible to the original vocabulary.
  • Name matching. Exact matching invented gaps — Eric/Erik Porter, Krista Gallio/Christa Galleo, Sheila/Shelia Wood were all in the CRM. Loose matching is worse: Mark Jones and Mary Jones are equally close and different people. The matcher scores rather than decides, and refuses to guess on pairs nothing mechanical can separate.
  • Template pollution. The largest apparent cluster in the queue was 1,761 copies of one SDR call script whose text discusses decision makers. It matched every filter and described no real account.

Both processes are documented as reusable skills with runnable quality gates and a regression suite for the name matcher, so this can be run for other clients without rediscovering any of the above.
